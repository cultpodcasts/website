What this proves — and what it does not

  • Font independent. Text cannot leave its own element box, so each text style is bounded over the whole of that box rather than only where glyphs happen to land. Block copy fills the min(640px, 100%) column, so no title length, locale or font substitution can escape the result. Measured ink is reported alongside as a sanity check.
  • The model is read from the live render, not re-declared here. Band edges, tint stops, mask stops and mask-composite come from getComputedStyle(el, '::before') inside the rig, so it tracks the stylesheet. Only axis-aligned gradients and intersect compositing are modelled; anything else is reported as unmodelled and fails rather than being guessed at.
  • Deliberately pessimistic. .billboard__scrim and .billboard__vignette also darken the art but are excluded from the model, so the real background is always at least as dark as the number shown. .billboard__grain (7% soft-light noise) is disabled and excluded.
  • Uniform art only. A solid fill is the worst case for contrast, and it makes backdrop-filter irrelevant — blurring #fff still yields #fff — so the tint alone is measured. It does not model whether blur adequately suppresses detail (the artwork's own burnt-in title); that remains a visual judgement.
  • Not pixel readback. Backgrounds are composited analytically from computed styles. The "Isolate band" preview makes the render match the model so the two can be compared by eye.
  • Buttons and chips use plain harness markup, not Angular Material / app-subject-chip, so their box metrics differ from production. Their backgrounds come from the real stylesheet and are composited over the band.
  • Thresholds are WCAG 2.1 AA: 4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text (≥24px, or ≥18.66px at weight ≥700).
